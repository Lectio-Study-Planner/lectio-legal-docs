Furthermore, state data protection laws of the individual federal states may apply.

Security Measures

In accordance with the legal requirements, taking into account the state of the art, the cost of implementation, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing, as well as the varying likelihood and severity of the risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons, we implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure a level of protection appropriate to the risk.

These measures include, in particular, safeguarding the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data by controlling physical and electronic access to the data as well as access, input, disclosure, availability, and its segregation. Furthermore, we have established procedures to ensure the exercise of data subject rights, the deletion of data, and responses to any compromise of data. We also take the protection of personal data into account as early as the development or selection of hardware, software, and procedures, in accordance with the principle of data protection through technology design and through data protection-friendly default settings.

Securing online connections through TLS/SSL encryption technology (HTTPS): To protect the data of users transmitted via our online services from unauthorized access, we use TLS/SSL encryption technology. Secure Sockets Layer (SSL) and Transport Layer Security (TLS) are the cornerstones of secure data transmission on the internet. These technologies encrypt the information transmitted between the website or app and the user's browser (or between two servers), thereby protecting the data from unauthorized access. TLS, as the more advanced and secure version of SSL, ensures that all data transmissions meet the highest security standards. When a website is secured with an SSL/TLS certificate, this is indicated by the display of HTTPS in the URL. This serves as an indicator to users that their data is being transmitted securely and encrypted.

Semester Sync

Without a user account, Lectio stores all of your semester data only on your device. If you are signed in, we also store it on our servers (Supabase, see "Third-Party Providers Used") so that it is the same on all of your devices. The following is synced:

  • Your semesters with all their contents: courses, readings, tasks and other items with their tags, due dates and reminder settings; the class schedule with classes, rooms and notes, and your attendance; your study time with the individual study sessions; vacations; your courses' credits, grades and partial grades; links to Moodle courses (see "Moodle Integration") and to TUCaN modules (see "TUCaN Import").
  • Your credit plan (table credit_plans): your degree's total credits, categories, statuses, grading scale and how the grade point average is calculated.
  • Your shared tags (table tag_libraries): the reading, task and attendance tags that all of your semesters can use.

This data can only be retrieved with your user account. Your friends see only the parts of your class schedule that you share (see "Username and Friends"). A change that has not yet reached our servers, for example because you are offline, waits on your device until it is uploaded.

What does not leave your device: Without a user account, your semesters, your credit plan and your tags are not transferred to us. Lectio then connects only when you start it yourself: to send feedback (see "Third-Party Providers Used") or to retrieve data directly from your university (Moodle, TUCaN). It also asks the App Store for updates (see "Update Check"). Even with a user account, the following stays on your device: your Moodle token, the app's settings on this device, which friends you show on your schedule, and the data that widgets and reminders are made from.

  • Types of data processed: Content data (your semesters, your credit plan and your shared tags with the contents listed above); account data (your user ID, the time of the last change).
  • Data subjects: Users (e.g., users of online services).
  • Purposes of processing and legitimate interests: Provision of contractual services and fulfillment of contractual obligations.
  • Retention and deletion: A semester is stored until you delete it; whatever you delete within a semester is also deleted from our servers with the next sync. The credit plan and the shared tags are stored until you delete your user account. Deleting the user account deletes all of this data from our servers. A waiting change is deleted from your device once it has been uploaded or you delete your user account.
  • Legal bases: Performance of a contract and pre-contractual inquiries (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Moodle Integration

Connecting the app to your university's Moodle site is optional. You pick your university from a list built into the app, or enter the address of the Moodle site. Searching the list transfers nothing; the app checks an address you enter with a request to that site that contains nothing about you.

Usually you connect by signing in on your Moodle's own sign-in page, which the system opens in a browser window. Your password goes directly to your university; Lectio never sees it. Moodle then issues the app a personal access token, as it does for the official Moodle app. If your university does not allow this sign-in, you can paste a token instead that you create in your Moodle profile.

The token is stored only in the protected keychain of this device; it is never synced through iCloud Keychain or transferred to us or to another device. The connection itself, that is the site address and your name in Moodle, is also stored only on this device.

The app retrieves your name, your courses and their contents directly from your Moodle site: when you import or sync courses, and for linked courses also by itself when the app opens, to find new material. These requests do not go through us. What you import becomes part of your semester, and so that syncing with Moodle works on all of your devices, so do the site address, the ids and names of the linked Moodle courses, and the ids and links of the material imported and skipped. If you are signed in, this is synced with the semester (see "Semester Sync").

"Open in Moodle" opens a Moodle page in the Moodle app if it is installed, otherwise in the browser; the Moodle app receives the page's address.

You can disconnect Moodle at any time in the app under Profile, which deletes the token from this device. The Moodle site is operated by your university, whose own privacy policy applies to it.

  • Types of data processed: Account data (your name and user ID in Moodle, the site address, the access token); content data (names, ids and contents of your courses, links to Moodle material).
  • Data subjects: Users (e.g., users of online services).
  • Purposes of processing and legitimate interests: Provision of contractual services and fulfillment of contractual obligations.
  • Retention and deletion: The token and the connection are stored only on this device until you disconnect Moodle or delete the app. Imported material and links to Moodle courses are deleted with the semester they belong to.
  • Legal bases: Performance of a contract and pre-contractual inquiries (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

TUCaN Import

Importing grades and credits from TUCaN, TU Darmstadt's campus management system, is optional. Lectio offers it mainly when you are connected to TU Darmstadt's Moodle, and imports only when you start the import yourself.

To import, you sign in on TU Darmstadt's own sign-in pages, which Lectio shows in a browser view of its own. Your password and your second factor go directly to TU Darmstadt; Lectio does not read or store them. The browser view keeps no data: cookies and other website data are discarded as soon as it closes.

After you sign in, Lectio receives only TUCaN's session cookie. With it, the app retrieves your transcript of records and your module results, and then signs you out of TUCaN. The cookie and the retrieved pages are held only in memory, are not transferred to us, and are discarded as soon as you close the import. The pages also contain details such as your name; Lectio takes only the data listed below and shows you what it would take before anything is saved.

What you take over becomes part of your semesters and your credit plan: module names and numbers, grades, credits, whether a module was passed, and the areas of your degree programme. If you are signed in, it is synced like all semester content (see "Semester Sync"). TU Darmstadt's privacy policy applies to the sign-in and to TUCaN's pages.

  • Types of data processed: Account data (TUCaN's session cookie, only during the import); content data (the names and numbers of your modules, the semester in which they were completed, grades, credits, whether they were passed, the areas of your degree programme).
  • Data subjects: Users (e.g., users of online services).
  • Purposes of processing and legitimate interests: Provision of contractual services and fulfillment of contractual obligations.
  • Retention and deletion: The session cookie and the retrieved pages are discarded as soon as you close the import. Imported data is deleted with the semester it belongs to, and in the credit plan when you remove it there or delete your user account.
  • Legal bases: Performance of a contract and pre-contractual inquiries (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Username and Friends

If you have a user account, you can choose a username; this is optional. Without a username, nobody can find you, and you cannot add friends. With one, other signed-in users can find you by it: as soon as someone types at least three characters, the app shows up to five usernames that begin with them. Only the username is shown, never your email address or your name. A username that you give up, by changing it or by deleting your user account, stays reserved for seven days and can then be chosen by someone else. The app refuses offensive usernames; we may also remove a username later if it breaks our terms of use.

You can send other users a friend request, which they can accept or decline. You can withdraw a request and remove a friend at any time. Requests and friendships are stored on our servers together with the user IDs of both accounts and are visible only to the two accounts concerned.

By accepting a friend request, you and your friend agree to see each other's class schedule. Under "What Friends See" (in the app's Friends sheet) you choose what is shared: lectures, exercise groups, exams and other classes; the names of your semesters, courses and classes; rooms; and the notes you write on individual classes. The start date and number of weeks of your semesters are used to place the classes in the calendar. Your attendance, readings, tasks, grades, credits and study time are never shared. Your friend's app retrieves the shared schedule from our servers when it is displayed and does not store it permanently. Sharing ends as soon as either of you removes the friendship. Which friends you have chosen to show on your schedule is stored only on your device.

You can block another user. A block ends any friendship and deletes all requests between you; neither of you then finds the other in the search, and the blocked user cannot send you requests. The blocked user is not told. You can also report a user, for example for an offensive username or unwanted requests. A report contains your user ID, the reported user's ID and username, the reason you chose and any details you add. Only we can read reports, and we review them within 24 hours; the reported user is not told who reported them. We use reports to protect users of the app and to take action against misuse, for example by removing a username or deleting an account.

  • Types of data processed: Account data (username, if chosen, user ID, the time the username was last changed); content data (friend requests and friendships, your sharing settings, the shared parts of your class schedule, blocked users, reports and the details they contain).
  • Data subjects: Users (e.g., users of online services).
  • Purposes of processing and legitimate interests: Provision of contractual services and fulfillment of contractual obligations; security measures (protection against misuse and harassment).
  • Retention and deletion: The username is stored until you change it, we remove it, or you delete your user account; a username you give up then stays reserved for seven days, without any link to your account. Friend requests are deleted once they are accepted, declined or withdrawn, friendships once either of you removes them, and both when either account is deleted. A block is stored until you lift it or either account is deleted. A report is deleted once we have handled it, at the latest after 12 months, and when the reported account is deleted; if you delete your own account, your reports are kept without your user ID.
  • Legal bases: Performance of a contract and pre-contractual inquiries (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) in protecting users from misuse, for reports.

Glossary of Terms

This section provides an overview of the terms used in this privacy policy. Where terms are defined by law, their statutory definitions apply. The following explanations are intended primarily to aid understanding.

  • Account data: Account data includes essential information necessary for identifying and managing contractual partners, user accounts, profiles, and similar associations. This data may include personal and demographic information such as names, contact information (addresses, phone numbers, email addresses), dates of birth, and specific identifiers (user IDs). Account data forms the basis for any formal interaction between individuals and services, institutions, or systems by enabling unique identification and communication.
  • Content data: Content data includes information generated in the course of creating, editing, and publishing content of all kinds. This category of data may include text, images, videos, audio files, and other multimedia content published across various platforms and media. Content data is not limited to the actual content itself, but also includes metadata that provides information about the content, such as tags, descriptions, author information, and publication dates.
  • Contact data: Contact data is essential information that enables communication with individuals or organizations. It includes, among other things, telephone numbers, postal addresses, and email addresses, as well as means of communication such as social media handles and instant messaging identifiers.
  • Meta, communication, and procedural data: Meta, communication, and procedural data are categories that contain information about how data is processed, transmitted, and managed. Metadata, also known as data about data, includes information that describes the context, origin, and structure of other data. It may include information on file size, creation date, document author, and revision history. Communication data captures the exchange of information between users via various channels, such as email traffic, call logs, social network messages, and chat histories, including the persons involved, timestamps, and transmission paths. Procedural data describes the processes and workflows within systems or organizations, including workflow documentation, transaction and activity logs, and audit logs used to track and verify processes.
  • Usage data: Usage data refers to information that records how users interact with digital products, services, or platforms. This data includes a wide range of information showing how users use applications, which features they prefer, how long they spend on certain pages, and which paths they take through an application. Usage data may also include frequency of use, activity timestamps, IP addresses, device information, and location data. It is particularly valuable for analyzing user behavior, optimizing user experiences, personalizing content, and improving products or services. In addition, usage data plays a crucial role in identifying trends, preferences, and potential problem areas within digital offerings.
  • Personal data: "Personal data" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (hereinafter "data subject"); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier (e.g., a cookie), or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person.
  • Log data: Log data is information about events or activities logged within a system or network. This data typically includes information such as timestamps, IP addresses, user actions, error messages, and other details about the use or operation of a system. Log data is often used to analyze system issues, monitor security, or generate performance reports.
  • Controller: The "controller" refers to the natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data.
  • Processing: "Processing" is any operation or set of operations performed on personal data, whether or not by automated means. The term is broad and encompasses virtually any handling of data, whether collecting, evaluating, storing, transmitting, or deleting it.
